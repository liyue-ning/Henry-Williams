Civilize before Christianizing
In terms of the early spread of the Gospel in Aotearoa New Zealand, I believe that Henry Williams was a significant individual. Though I did not research nearly as much about other missionaries, I think that Williams was significant due to his different approach to Sammuel Marsden which overtime, was successful and arguably more effective. I strongly agree with Henry William's approach instead of Sammuel Marsden's as Marsden believed in civilizing the Māori before 'Christianizing' them (which is why the CMS philosophy initially was "civilize before Christianizing") which meant that he believed that Māori need to learn about European culture, industry, and agriculture in order for them to understand the Gospel. To me this isn't necessary because anyone can learn the word of God—the Gospel does not discriminate. By teaching the Māori European knowledge, Christianity would seem like it was only available to those who learnt it. This is not true, and it would break my heart if some truly believed that. This contrasts to Williams' method which involved direct evangelism. He argued that the Gospel should be taught immediately and direct through scripture. I agree with his method because I believe that Marsden was driving away from the mission it was almost like he was prioritizing civilization rather than putting God in the center. Furthermore, the fact that he was influential is evidence of his significance as it shows how the Māori saw him as a peacemaker—reflecting patience and forgiveness of God—and how he was able to contribute to the translation of the Treaty of Waitangi which is important because Williams was able to introduce the idea of a covenant—a binding, agreement, relationship established between humanity and God.

Conversion
Without Henry Williams, I would argue that a lot less natives would have been baptized and learnt the word of God due to his method of spiritual teachings instead of Sammuel's method. As a result, by prioritizing the spread of the Gospel and learning about the word, the natives would be able to spread it themselves at an earlier rate. His significance also is highlighted through other actions such as independently baptizing more than 1000 Māori by 1844. Which I think is incredibly impressive for a single individual, because not only may a religion be new and unfamiliar to them, but Williams also had to be patience and have themselves choose God independently, resulting in a significant amount of effort and time spent with the natives. This heavily contrast to Marsden success which was actually 0. During Marsden time in charge—between 1814 and 1823 which is nearly a decade—the mission failed to produce a single Māori conversion to Christianity. I honestly think it's rather disappointing that even after 10 years being with Māori they were unable to officially baptize or convert anyone, because the fact that already the religious messages behind Christianity was largely ignored and Marsden could not get Māori to embrace Christian faith seems very telling of his philosophy. This may have been the result of prioritizing education about the Europeans rather than the word of God itself. Moreover, because of Williams public baptism of Rawiri Taiwhanga, that the 1830s saw a massive increase in Māori interest in Christianity. At the time, conversion was viewed as a threat to chiefs because they valued their prestige and spiritual authority (mana). Consequently, accepting God meant they had to abandon traditional tapu/scared restrictions and ancestral spiritual protections. It was the actions and direction of the CMS lead by Williams that Taiwhanga was baptized, leading to a huge break in momentum and additionally breaking the social stigma that conversion and believing in God would degrade a chief's mana. Had another missionary take charge and strictly followed the old philosophy: "civilize before Christianizing", Māori would have likely continued their disinterest towards Christianity, or the increase would have been at a slower rate.

Language + Peace
The early spread of the Gospel impacted the community because it introduced literacy as the Early Church Missionary Society (CMS) developed a written alphabet and dictionary for the Māori as well as introducing the printing press, which allowed the New Testament to be spread at a quicker rate. I think this really benefited the community because not only did this allow Williams to appreciate a different culture, but Māori were also able to share the Gospel in written forms as well as all missionaries being able to translate the scripture more accurately. Due to Williams introducing the idea of literacy to the Māori, the community started becoming interesting in the power of reading and writing, which resulted in the reading of scriptures becoming a huge cultural attraction. Therefore, in terms of conversion, Williams was far more significant and impactful as baptism during his time sparked widespread Māori adoption of Christianity. This contrast to Marsden who heavily relied on interpreters which mean that the Gospel was less accurate and they may have fallen under communication barriers. Moreover, the Gospel was a tool for peace evident in how the Christian message of forgiveness was used to eventually end the Musket Wars (it lasted for decades). This meant that Williams had to actively risk his life during these wars in order to show God's act of forgiveness. In addition, the early spread of the Gospel lead to the freeing of slaves, which means that if they had not been present, it would have been likely these positive outcomes would have occurred at a much later rate.
